# Arcten Inference: GLM‑5.2 FP8 feasibility

**Research date:** August 1, 2026

## 1. Executive conclusion

**Do not publish the proposed rate card as a binding commitment.**

The current evidence supports a limited **Priority** and **Standard** beta, using average completion targets and admission control. **Flex** is viable for output tokens but its input and cached-input prices are fragile. **Now** is not sustainable as a universal, low-latency exact-FP8 service at the proposed output price.

The exact checkpoint is a 78-layer sparse MoE with 256 routed experts, 8 active per token, one native MTP layer, and a 1,048,576-token context limit. FP8, BF16, and NVFP4 are separate deployments; NVFP4 is explicitly a different quantized checkpoint. SGLang also defaults the KV cache to FP8 on Blackwell and BF16 on Hopper, so merely using the same weight files does not ensure the same serving precision. ([SGLang Documentation][1])

The proposed **Now** prices exactly match Z.ai’s public GLM‑5.2 API rate: $1.40 input, $0.26 cached input, and $4.40 output per million tokens. Z.ai does not establish that its retail endpoint uses Arcten’s exact proposed checkpoint, precision, kernels, or latency contract, so this is a market-price ceiling rather than a fully apples-to-apples cost comparison. ([Overview - Z.AI DEVELOPER DOCUMENT][2])

### Tier verdict

The following uses the official 8×B200 SGLang benchmark, Nebius B200 prices of $57.20/node-hour on demand and $31.60 preemptible, and explicit utilization/overhead assumptions. The input/output split is a provisional latency-weighted allocation, not isolated prefill/decode measurement. ([Nebius AI Cloud][3])

| Tier         |                      Assumed operating point | Published TTFT / derived completion | Loaded input / output COGS | Proposed price | Verdict                                                                         |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------: | ----------------------------------: | -------------------------: | -------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Now**      |              c64, on demand, 85% utilization |                       5.74s / 23.8s |          **$0.24 / $5.96** |  $1.40 / $4.40 | Output loses money; latency is not especially immediate                         |
| **Priority** |           c256, preemptible, 85% utilization |                      18.74s / 52.1s |          **$0.14 / $1.93** |  $0.70 / $3.00 | Plausible, but speculative acceptance was simulated and there is no tail result |
| **Standard** | c1024 arrivals, preemptible, 90% utilization |                    177.62s / 226.7s |          **$0.34 / $0.76** |  $0.50 / $2.50 | Plausible; input margin is thin                                                 |
| **Flex**     | c1024 arrivals, preemptible, 95% utilization |                    177.62s / 226.7s |          **$0.33 / $0.72** |  $0.40 / $1.80 | Output viable; uncached input below a 30% gross-margin target                   |

The c1–c256 benchmark cells use `SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN`, fixing MTP acceptance at 3.5 for low latency and 2 for balanced serving. They are therefore optimistic simulations of speculative performance, not measured GLM‑5.2 acceptance. The c1024 high-throughput point disables speculation and is the strongest public economic evidence. None of the points reports P95/P99 latency, GPU power, utilization, or actual HBM occupancy. ([SGLang Documentation][1])

For a standalone **30% gross margin**, the modeled output price floors are approximately:

| Tier     | Output COGS | 30% GM price floor | Proposed output |
| -------- | ----------: | -----------------: | --------------: |
| Now      |       $5.96 |          **$8.52** |           $4.40 |
| Priority |       $1.93 |          **$2.75** |           $3.00 |
| Standard |       $0.76 |          **$1.09** |           $2.50 |
| Flex     |       $0.72 |          **$1.03** |           $1.80 |

The Now tier can still make money on sufficiently prompt-heavy traffic because expensive input revenue cross-subsidizes decode. Under the sustained c64 scenario, it requires roughly **1.34 uncached input tokens per output token merely to break even**, before cached-input dilution. Coding and reasoning traffic can have the opposite mix.

### Commercial recommendation

* Launch **Priority** and **Standard** as target windows, not strict SLAs, after reproducing P95/P99 results.
* Do not launch universal **Now** at $4.40 output. Restrict it by token mix/output cap, charge materially more for output, or omit it.
* Make **Flex cached input** conditional on TTL, cache residency, and reuse rather than a flat $0.08.
* Treat any NVFP4, modified-backbone, expert-pruned, Medusa‑2, diffusion, or hybrid model as a separate SKU.
* Use the MIT checkpoint for self-hosting. Do not assume Z.ai or another provider’s retail API may be resold. BYOK belongs to Gateway; OEM or wholesale capacity requires a separate contract. ([Hugging Face][4])

## 2. Exactness boundary

| Category                     | Meaning                                                                                                               | Operational implication                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bitwise identity**         | Identical output bits for the same request and seed                                                                   | Requires pinned hardware, topology, kernels, batching behavior, RNG consumption, compiler and engine. Generally unrealistic across fleet changes.    |
| **Mathematical equivalence** | Same function in exact real arithmetic                                                                                | Parallel reductions and floating-point order may still change logits or ties. Expert parallelism may fall here, but not FP8↔BF16 KV substitution.    |
| **Distribution-preserving**  | Samples follow the target model’s probability distribution, although individual sequences or seed behavior may differ | Canonical speculative rejection/residual sampling can satisfy this. It is the appropriate exactness claim for stochastic decoding.                   |
| **Lossy approximation**      | Logits or the target distribution change                                                                              | Quantization changes, relaxed acceptance, target fine-tuning, expert pruning/dropping, altered routing, and direct diffusion generation belong here. |

Canonical speculative decoding accepts draft tokens according to the target/draft probability ratio and samples from a residual distribution after rejection. Under correct implementation, this preserves the target distribution exactly; it does not guarantee bitwise identity or identical RNG trajectories. ([arXiv][5])

### Exact GLM‑5.2 FP8 SKU manifest

Arcten should bind each SKU to:

* Model repository and immutable revision/hash.
* Tokenizer files, vocabulary and chat template.
* `generation_config`, reasoning mode and parser behavior.
* 1,048,576-token context cap.
* FP8 checkpoint and exact FP8 execution configuration.
* KV-cache dtype.
* Engine, kernel, CUDA and compiler versions.
* Sampling semantics, including supported temperature, top-p, top-k, penalties and seed behavior.
* Tool-call and reasoning parsers.

This matters because the official SGLang deployment currently chooses FP8 KV on Blackwell and BF16 KV on Hopper, and uses separate reasoning and tool-call parsers. Those are observable serving differences despite an identical model repository name. ([SGLang Documentation][1])

### Main technical conclusion

The only class considered here that can reduce decode cost while preserving the exact target distribution is:

> **An auxiliary drafting method followed by canonical verification against the unmodified GLM‑5.2 FP8 target.**

EAGLE, MTP, Medusa‑1, DSpark and DFlash can satisfy that condition when their verifier and sampler are exact. The draft itself need not approximate the target perfectly; poor drafts reduce speed rather than quality.

Direct diffusion generation, hybrid AR–diffusion targets, Medusa‑2, target fine-tuning, expert pruning and alternate quantization require a separate SKU.

Derived completion is:

[
T_{\text{completion}} = TTFT + (1024-1)\times TPOT
]

It is not a tail-latency estimate.

### 4.2 Raw and loaded COGS

Let:

* (P): 8-GPU node price per hour.
* (T): published total tokens/s/GPU.
* (c): concurrency.
* (u): effective sold utilization after idle and warm-tail capacity.
* (M): non-idle overhead multiplier.

Raw blended GPU cost:

[
C_{\text{blend}}
================

\frac{P}{8T\cdot3600}\times10^6
]

Provisional stage allocations:

[
C^0_{\text{input}}
==================

\frac{P\cdot TTFT}{c\cdot8192\cdot3600}\times10^6
]

[
C^0_{\text{output}}
===================

\frac{P\cdot1023\cdot TPOT}{c\cdot1024\cdot3600}\times10^6
]

Both are scaled by a common (k) so their 8:1 token-weighted average equals the observed blended GPU cost:

[
k=
\frac{C_{\text{blend}}}
{\frac{8192C^0_{\text{input}}+1024C^0_{\text{output}}}{9216}}
]

Loaded cost:

[
C_{\text{loaded}} = kC^0\frac{M}{u}
]

In this planning model:

* (u) captures idle and warm-tail capacity.
* (M) captures CPU, RAM, storage, network, orchestration, model load/compilation, retries, interruption reserve, provider overhead and payment overhead.
* On demand uses (M=1.15).
* Preemptible uses (M=1.30).

These multipliers are assumptions. Production accounting must replace them with measured line items.

### 4.5 Cached-input economics

The GLM config specifies:

* `kv_lora_rank = 512`
* `qk_rope_head_dim = 64`
* 78 hidden layers. ([Hugging Face][12])

For a one-byte FP8 cache, a lower-bound compressed KV footprint is:

[
(512+64)\times78 = 44{,}928\ \text{bytes/token}
]

Therefore:

[
1\text{M cached tokens} \approx 44.928\text{ GB per cache replica}
]

This excludes scales, block metadata, index state, allocator fragmentation and other engine overhead.

A planning cache-hit cost is:

[
C_{\text{cache}}
================

C_{\text{lookup}}
+
\frac{
44.928 \cdot r \cdot h \cdot C_{\text{HBM}}
}{n}
]

where:

* (r): cache replication factor.
* (h): average residency in hours.
* (n): number of paid reuse hits.
* (C_{\text{HBM}}=$0.06/\text{GB-hour}), an assumed opportunity cost.
* (C_{\text{lookup}}=$0.02/\text{MTok-hit}), assumed.

| Reuse case   | Residency / paid hits |   r=1 COGS | r=8 COGS |
| ------------ | --------------------: | ---------: | -------: |
| High reuse   |            5 min / 10 | **$0.043** |   $0.200 |
| Medium reuse |            10 min / 5 | **$0.110** |   $0.739 |
| Low reuse    |            30 min / 2 | **$0.694** |   $5.411 |

Implications:

* Flex’s **$0.08** works only with approximately one cache owner and high reuse.
* Standard’s **$0.12** barely supports the one-replica medium-reuse case.
* Replicated KV makes every proposed cached-input price fail.
* Cold misses and evictions must be charged as uncached input.
* Host-memory hierarchical caching can reduce HBM residency, but adds RAM, transfers and lookup latency to COGS. SGLang exposes this option for long-context workloads. ([SGLang Documentation][1])
* Z.ai’s cached-input storage is currently listed as “limited-time free,” which is not a sustainable cost baseline. ([Overview - Z.AI DEVELOPER DOCUMENT][2])

The commercial design should be either:

1. A cache-hit price plus storage rent, or
2. A cached price conditioned on a maximum TTL and minimum reuse.

### 4.6 Workload-distribution sensitivity

Assuming one-replica medium-reuse cache COGS of $0.110/MTok:

| Workload          | Token mix                                 |   Now GM | Priority GM | Standard GM |  Flex GM |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------: | ----------: | ----------: | -------: |
| Chat              | 2K uncached input, 1K output              |      11% |         50% |         59% |      47% |
| Cache-heavy agent | 64K logical input, 90% cached, 400 output |      60% |         50% |         20% | **−10%** |
| Coding            | 8K input, 25% cached, 8K output           | **−12%** |         42% |         64% |      53% |

This exposes two adverse-selection risks:

* **Now attracts output-heavy workloads**, where its output price is below COGS.
* **Flex attracts cache-heavy workloads**, where its cached-input price can be below storage opportunity cost.

### Reproduction code

```python
ROWS = {
    1:    (0.757,   0.00322, 288),
    16:   (3.188,   0.00912, 1476),
    64:   (5.742,   0.01765, 3078),
    256:  (18.744,  0.03261, 5022),
    1024: (177.620, 0.04799, 4059),
}

def stage_costs(
    node_price: float,
    concurrency: int,
    ttft_s: float,
    tpot_s: float,
    tps_per_gpu: float,
    utilization: float = 1.0,
    overhead: float = 1.0,
) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Return input, output and blended $/MTok."""
    blended = node_price / (8 * tps_per_gpu * 3600) * 1e6

    input_pre = (
        node_price * ttft_s
        / (concurrency * 8192 * 3600)
        * 1e6
    )
    output_pre = (
        node_price * (1023 * tpot_s)
        / (concurrency * 1024 * 3600)
        * 1e6
    )

    weighted_pre = (
        8192 * input_pre + 1024 * output_pre
    ) / 9216
    scale = blended / weighted_pre

    load = overhead / utilization
    return (
        scale * input_pre * load,
        scale * output_pre * load,
        blended * load,
    )

# Example: Priority, Nebius preemptible 8xB200.
print(stage_costs(
    node_price=31.60,
    concurrency=256,
    ttft_s=ROWS[256][0],
    tpot_s=ROWS[256][1],
    tps_per_gpu=ROWS[256][2],
    utilization=0.85,
    overhead=1.30,
))
```

## 6. What could become proprietary

### 6.1 Deadline-aware economic controller

The strongest defensible layer is a controller that jointly chooses:

* Admission, start time and queue deadline.
* Batch composition and batch width.
* Prefill versus decode placement.
* Cache owner, affinity, TTL and eviction priority.
* Whether to speculate.
* Draft method, draft length and verification budget.
* Expert placement and load-balancing state.
* Spot interruption exposure and fallback reserve.
* Cancellation and retry policy.

The controller should optimize expected contribution margin subject to a completion-time distribution, not maximize raw tokens/s.

### 6.2 Exactness certification

A test system covering:

* Greedy equivalence.
* Distributional tests for temperature, top-p and top-k.
* Penalties, stop conditions and logprobs.
* Tool calling and structured output.
* Reasoning mode.
* Kernel, engine and topology changes.
* Retry and interrupted-request behavior.

This could become commercially valuable because “same model” is otherwise underspecified.

### 6.3 Target-specific adaptive drafting

A GLM‑specific DSpark/DFlash-style drafter plus an online controller trained on Arcten’s actual request distribution could be defensible. The public algorithms are not proprietary; the combination of:

* Customer workload traces.
* Acceptance prediction.
* Load-aware draft-length control.
* Ragged verification kernels.
* Deadline and cache state.
* MoE expert skew.
* Fleet interruption risk.

could be.

## 7. Three experiments within two weeks

### Experiment 1 — Exact GLM‑5.2 speculative serving matrix

This is the recommended two-week serving experiment.

**Configuration**

* 8×B200.
* Immutable `zai-org/GLM-5.2-FP8` revision.
* FP8 KV cache.
* SGLang 0.5.16 and vLLM 0.26.0.
* Baselines:

  * No speculation.
  * Native MTP, draft lengths 1–5.
  * GLM DSpark preview, draft lengths 1–7.
* Concurrency: 1, 16, 64, 256 and a queued 1,024-arrival test.
* Workloads:

  * 1K input / 256 output.
  * 8K / 1K.
  * 64K / 400.
  * 8K / 8K.
  * Representative coding, agent, chat and reasoning traces.

**Measure**

* P50/P95/P99 TTFT, TPOT and completion.
* Prefill and decode tokens/s separately.
* GPU power, SM utilization and memory bandwidth.
* HBM/KV occupancy.
* Expert skew and all-to-all time.
* Draft latency, verification latency and accepted length by position.
* Acceptance by workload and context length.
* Loaded dollars per input/output million tokens.

**Exactness**

* Greedy output comparison under a pinned topology.
* Statistical distribution tests under temperature/top-p/top-k.
* Only canonical exact rejection/residual sampling.
* No relaxed or “typical” acceptance.

**Gate**

Proceed only if the method produces:

* At least **15% lower loaded decode COGS at c64 and c256**.
* No more than 5% P99 latency regression.
* No distribution-test failure.
* Automatic disabling when speculation is unfavorable.

### Experiment 2 — Deadline-aware scheduler replay

Compare:

1. FIFO continuous batching.
2. Deadline-first scheduling.
3. Deadline + cache affinity.
4. Deadline + adaptive batch width + admission control.

Replay low-load, burst, sustained and output-heavy traces. Inject cancellations and correlated arrivals.

**Gate**

* Lower COGS at the same P99 completion-target miss rate.
* No starvation of Now/Priority traffic.
* Positive contribution margin under the actual token mix.
* Demonstrated improvement beyond stock SGLang/vLLM scheduling.

### Experiment 3 — Spot, cache and recovery chaos test

Repeatedly:

* Interrupt a node during prefill and decode.
* Evict prefix KV.
* Restart after model load or compilation.
* Fail a KV-transfer path.
* Trigger duplicate retries and client disconnects.
* Force fallback from spot to on-demand.

Measure reload time, stranded compute, duplicate billing exposure, lost KV value, warm-pool requirements and P99 completion impact.

**Gate**

Produce measured values for:

* Failure and retry reserve.
* Required warm capacity.
* Effective spot discount after interruption.
* Cache replication factor.
* Cache TTL/reuse economics.
* Idempotency guarantees.

No Flex or spot-backed SLA should be priced before this experiment.

## 8. Three-month engineering recommendation

### Month 1 — Establish the truth baseline

* Pin the exact SKU manifest.
* Reproduce the 8×B200 benchmark without simulated acceptance.
* Add stage-specific prefill/decode instrumentation.
* Build the exactness test suite.
* Measure cache memory, reuse and residency.
* Implement line-item COGS accounting.
* Run all three two-week experiments.

**Commercial state:** no binding price or deadline commitments.

### Month 2 — Build the controller

* Deadline-aware admission and batching.
* Cache affinity and TTL-aware routing.
* Dynamic speculation on/off.
* Dynamic draft length.
* Spot/fallback reserve accounting.
* Cancellation-aware scheduling.
* Per-workload margin prediction.

**Commercial state:** invite-only Priority and Standard beta with target windows.

### Month 3 — Production hardening and research pilot

* Shadow traffic across multiple B200 providers.
* P99 deadline and failure testing.
* Ragged speculative verification.
* CUDA graph coverage for dynamic draft shapes.
* Expert-skew-aware placement.
* Cache spill and recovery.
* Billing and idempotency hardening.

**Commercial state:** publish prices only after measured workload-weighted gross margin remains positive under burst and failure tests.

### Recommended three-month IP project

**Exact adaptive drafting for sparse MoE serving**

Freeze GLM‑5.2 FP8 as the verifier and train a 3–5B semi-autoregressive or block-diffusion drafter on:

* Target hidden states.
* Target-generated traces.
* Arcten request domains.
* Acceptance survival labels.
* Context-length and cache-state features.

Train a confidence/survival head. At runtime, a controller chooses:

* Speculation on/off.
* Draft length and shape.
* Verification budget.
* Relevant target hidden layers.
* Tier deadline.
* Current batch load.
* Expert skew.
* Cache residency.
* Interruption risk.

Always retain canonical verification and a no-spec fallback.

**Three-month success gate**

* At least **20% lower loaded decode COGS at c64–c256** on held-out real traces.
* Target distribution tests pass.
* Less than 5% P99 regression.
* No material cache-hit-rate reduction.
* Gain persists across at least two providers or GPU configurations.

Failure to meet that gate should terminate custom-drafter work and shift effort to scheduling, cache economics and fleet control.

## 9. Explicit reasons the approach might fail

1. **Insufficient demand density.** Delayed requests only help if enough compatible work arrives. A warm node at c1 has catastrophic per-token economics.

2. **Immediate latency and low cost conflict.** The c64 point needed for tolerable Now economics already has 5.7-second TTFT and approximately 24-second completion for a 1K-token output.

3. **The Now output price is below modeled COGS.** Output-heavy coding and reasoning workloads create negative gross margin.

4. **The strongest Priority evidence is simulated.** Public c1–c256 MTP acceptance was fixed using an environment variable rather than measured from GLM‑5.2.

5. **Speculation may stop helping at production batch sizes.** Drafting and larger verification batches can consume more compute than they save; published EAGLE gains differ materially by engine and implementation.

6. **Long prompts are a different bottleneck.** Speculative decoding primarily accelerates decode. It does not solve long-context prefill cost or TTFT.

7. **MoE routing can produce stragglers.** Expert skew and all-to-all communication may dominate latency despite low active parameter count.

8. **Spot savings can disappear after failures.** Lost KV, retries, reloads, duplicated work, fallback capacity and warm reserves all reduce the apparent discount.

9. **Cached-input prices depend on reuse, TTL and topology.** A flat cache price transfers reuse and replication risk to Arcten.

10. **Strict 1- and 5-minute deadlines need reserve capacity.** The public benchmark reports no P95/P99 and leaves limited Standard headroom after its approximately 227-second central completion estimate.

11. **Adverse selection can invert tier margins.** Now attracts output-heavy work; Flex attracts cache-heavy or long-wait work.

12. **Customer cancellations strand compute.** Longer queues and long reasoning outputs increase the chance that already-spent prefill or decode is never billable.

13. **Numerical and sampler differences can violate the product claim.** Different KV precision, kernels, parser versions or relaxed speculative acceptance can change observable behavior.

14. **Engine and kernel churn is rapid.** Current releases and recipes can introduce performance or correctness regressions, particularly across new GPU generations.

15. **A separate quantized model may appear cheaper but is not equivalent.** NVFP4, expert pruning or modified routing cannot be used to satisfy an exact FP8 request.

16. **Full diffusion economics are irrelevant to the exact SKU.** Their speedups come from separate training objectives and model distributions.

17. **The public retail price may be structurally difficult to beat.** Z.ai may have ownership, hardware, volume, or strategic-pricing advantages unavailable to Arcten.

18. **Retail API arbitrage may be contractually unavailable.** Self-hosting open weights, BYOK routing, OEM capacity and retail API resale are different legal and economic arrangements.

## Bottom line

Arcten’s thesis is **conditionally correct**:

> Waiting can reduce cost when it increases sold utilization, improves output batching, and permits cheaper capacity without causing excessive retries, cache cost or deadline reserve.

Waiting itself creates no saving.

The likely viable initial product is **exact GLM‑5.2 FP8 Priority and Standard serving with average completion targets, workload-aware admission control, and conditional cache pricing**. The proposed universal Now tier and unconditional Flex cached-input price are not supported by current evidence.

The strongest defensible opportunity is not another public serving kernel. It is an **exactness-certified, deadline-, cache-, speculation-, MoE- and interruption-aware economic controller**, potentially paired with a GLM-specific adaptive drafter.
