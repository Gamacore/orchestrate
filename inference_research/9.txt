# GLM‑5.2 FP8 competitive economics

**Public-information reconstruction as of August 1, 2026.** Prices are per million tokens unless noted.

## 1. Executive conclusion

Arcten’s thesis is directionally correct: a longer completion window can reduce cost by increasing productive utilization, admitting work only when capacity is cheap, and tolerating interruption. It does **not** make sequential decode arbitrarily cheap. For GLM‑5.2 FP8, output-token economics remain the binding constraint.

The exact public reference is `zai-org/GLM-5.2-FP8`: approximately 743B total parameters, 39B active parameters, and a 1,048,576-token context. The FP8 checkpoint is roughly 756 GB. FP4, NVFP4, shorter-context, or ambiguously versioned services are not exact comparators. ([vLLM Recipes][1])

Using:

* An exact public 8×H200 GLM‑5.2-FP8 benchmark.
* Public H200 rental prices.
* A third-party owner/hyperscaler H200 TCO proxy.
* Explicit reserves for non-GPU infrastructure, failures, and orchestration.

The modeled result is:

| Arcten tier  | Proposed input / cached / output | Modeled output COGS at owner-scale, 85% utilization | Output gross margin | Recommendation                                        |
| ------------ | -------------------------------: | --------------------------------------------------: | ------------------: | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Now**      |            $1.40 / $0.26 / $4.40 |                                               $2.50 |                 43% | Plausible with wholesale or owner-equivalent capacity |
| **Priority** |            $0.70 / $0.18 / $3.00 |                                               $2.50 |                 17% | Pilot only; little failure or demand-mix tolerance    |
| **Standard** |            $0.50 / $0.12 / $2.50 |                                               $2.50 |    Approximately 0% | Do not commit at this price on H200                   |
| **Flex**     |            $0.40 / $0.08 / $1.80 |                                               $2.50 |                −39% | Unsustainable on the public H200 evidence             |

The modeled input COGS in the same sustained-load scenario is approximately **$0.159/M**. Cached-input COGS can be approximately **$0.070/M** at twenty successful cache reuses per prefix-hour, but this is an optimistic residency lower bound, not a generally achievable rate.

### Commercial verdict

**Prices Arcten can plausibly match**

* Fireworks, Novita, Z.AI, and Together’s headline **$1.40 input / $4.40 output** level—but only after Arcten obtains owner-equivalent or wholesale capacity and maintains high utilization. Together’s service is FP4 and therefore not an exact technical comparator. ([Fireworks AI][2])
* Sail ASAP at **$1.00 / $0.20 / $3.50**, with a thinner but plausible margin.
* GMICloud’s approximately **$0.924 / $0.172 / $2.904** OpenRouter route, but only near owner-scale utilization and with very little reserve.

**Prices Arcten should not match yet**

* Sail Standard at **$0.50 / $0.12 / $2.50**: approximately break-even under the optimistic owner-H200 case.
* Sail Flex at **$0.40 / $0.08 / $1.80**: below the modeled H200 output floor.
* StreamLake and Novita’s approximately 75%-discounted OpenRouter routes near **$1.11 output**: below even the public owner-H200 floor, and likely dependent on promotion, strategic channel economics, materially better hardware economics, or subsidy. ([Sail Research][3])
* DeepInfra’s $2.40 output and Baseten’s $4.40 output as evidence for FP8 economics: both publicly describe lower-precision execution. ([DeepInfra][4])

A B200 fleet may materially improve this result. SemiAnalysis reports B200 as 45% more cost-efficient than H200 at one FP8 GLM‑5/5.1 operating point, but this is not an exact GLM‑5.2 benchmark. Applying it only as an upside sensitivity gives roughly **$1.6–$2.1/M output** at owner scale. Arcten should not price against that projection until it reproduces it on the exact checkpoint. ([InferenceX][5])

### Commercial mode distinctions

* **Open-weight self-hosting:** Arcten operates the checkpoint and bears GPU, utilization, failure, privacy, and exactness risk. The model repository is MIT-licensed, but customer, infrastructure, data, and downstream-use obligations remain separate. ([Hugging Face][14])
* **Wholesale/OEM:** Arcten has negotiated resale or white-label rights, pricing, volume commitments, data terms, and SLAs.
* **BYOK:** The customer has the provider relationship and supplies its account or key. Arcten supplies routing and control-plane software.
* **Retail API resale:** A provider’s public API price does not establish sublicensing or resale rights.
* **Cloud credits and launch promotions:** Useful for experiments, but excluded from sustainable COGS.

## 3. Reproducible calculations

### 3.1 Exactness manifest

Before benchmarking or selling an “identical model,” pin:

```text
checkpoint repository + immutable commit
SHA-256 of every weight shard
tokenizer.json / tokenizer.model hashes
tokenizer_config.json
chat_template
config.json
generation_config.json
maximum prompt and completion limits
weight precision and quantization recipe
KV-cache dtype
engine/container image digest
CUDA, driver, NCCL, FlashInfer, CUTLASS versions
reasoning and tool-call parsers
sampling defaults, stop handling, and seed behavior
MTP/speculative decoding configuration
```

Quality categories:

| Category                     | Meaning                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bitwise identity**         | Same binaries, hardware, kernel paths, reduction order, seed, and execution schedule produce identical bytes. Generally unrealistic across distributed providers. |
| **Mathematical equivalence** | The same mathematical transformation is implemented, although floating-point reduction order may change low-order bits.                                           |
| **Distribution-preserving**  | Sampling from the same target distribution; target-verified speculative decoding can qualify even though outputs are not bitwise identical.                       |
| **Lossy approximation**      | FP4/NVFP4/MXFP4 weights, reduced-precision KV relative to a higher-precision reference, approximate attention, or model substitution.                             |

### 3.2 Benchmark basis

The public GPUStack benchmark uses:

* `zai-org/GLM-5.2-FP8`
* 8×NVIDIA H200
* vLLM 0.24.0 and SGLang 0.5.14
* TP8/DP8, DP attention, DeepEP, `max-running-requests=256`, and FP8 E4M3 KV in its optimized configuration. ([GPU Stack][15])

The current releases at the cutoff are vLLM 0.26.0 and SGLang 0.5.16, so the benchmark must be repeated both at its original pins and at current pinned images. ([GitHub][16])

Two workload-derived phase proxies are used:

```text
Prefill proxy
= 3,201,200 input tokens / 135.63 seconds
= 23,602 input tokens/second per 8×H200 node

Decode proxy
= 400,000 output tokens / 266.01 seconds
= 1,505.53 output tokens/second per 8×H200 node
```

The first comes from the optimized 32K-input/100-output case; the second comes from the 1K-input/2K-output case. These are workload proxies, not isolated kernel microbenchmarks, and therefore carry substantial model risk. ([GPU Stack][15])

### 3.3 Complete phase COGS formula

```text
Phase COGS per 1M tokens
= node price per hour
  × non-GPU/failure multiplier
  × 1,000,000
  ÷ (phase tokens/second × 3,600 × effective utilization)
```

The multiplier covers:

```text
model loading and compilation
CPU, RAM, storage, and networking
orchestration and monitoring
interruption, retry, and failure reserve
provider and payment overhead
```

It does not include sales, general R&D, or desired profit margin.

### 3.6 Cached-input economics

The model configuration contains 78 layers, a KV LoRA rank of 512, and a 64-dimensional RoPE component. An optimistic FP8 latent-state lower bound is:

```text
(512 + 64) × 78 × 1 byte
= 44,928 bytes per cached token
= 44.928 GB per million cached tokens
```

The real footprint can be higher because of index state, runtime buffers, allocator fragmentation, block metadata, duplication, and implementation details. 

Eight H200s provide 1,128 GB of HBM. Subtracting the approximately 756 GB checkpoint leaves an optimistic 372 GB before runtime allocations. ([Fireworks AI][8])

At the owner/wholesale node price:

```text
Residency cost per 1M prefix tokens per hour
= $9.76 × 1.18 × 44.928 / 372
= $1.391
```

| Successful cache uses per prefix-hour | Optimistic cached COGS |
| ------------------------------------: | ---------------------: |
|                                     5 |               $0.278/M |
|                                    10 |               $0.139/M |
|                                    20 |               $0.070/M |
|                                    50 |               $0.028/M |

Implications:

* **$0.12 cached input** needs approximately twelve or more successful uses per prefix-hour under this lower-bound model.
* **$0.08** needs approximately eighteen uses.
* On on-demand H200 capacity, the residency lower bound is $5.217/M-prefix-hour; even twenty uses cost approximately $0.261/M before miss processing and overhead.
* Caching cannot be priced as a fixed percentage of input independently of prefix size, residency time, hit rate, eviction, and tenant isolation.

### 3.7 Workload distributions

Using the sustained owner-scale costs of:

```text
Novel input:  $0.159/M
Cached input: $0.070/M, assuming 20 successful uses/prefix-hour
Output:       $2.500/M
```

| Workload distribution                      | Blended modeled COGS per M mixed tokens | Now GM | Priority GM | Standard GM |    Flex GM |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------: | -----: | ----------: | ----------: | ---------: |
| Long-agent, cold: 8 novel input : 1 output |                                  $0.420 |  75.8% |       56.1% |       41.9% |      24.5% |
| Cache-rich: 2 novel : 6 cached : 1 output  |                                  $0.360 |  63.1% |       40.9% |       23.3% |  **−5.1%** |
| Generation-heavy: 1 novel input : 2 output |                                  $1.720 |  49.4% |       23.0% |        6.2% | **−29.0%** |

A blended workload can make a loss-making output rate look acceptable when input dominates. Arcten should maintain separate contribution ledgers for novel input, cache residency, cache misses, and decode.

## 4. Optimization evidence

The savings ranges below are **engineering underwriting ranges**, not statistical confidence intervals. Public reports generally provide one or a few workload points without repeated-run distributions, power data, or complete fleet overhead.

### 4.1 SGLang serving configuration on 8×H200

**Quality category:** Mathematically equivalent for scheduling and parallelism changes; FP8 KV is lossy relative to BF16 KV. No bitwise guarantee.

**Applicability:** Exact GLM‑5.2-FP8, 8×H200, high-concurrency medium/long prompts.

**Reported results, 8K input / 1K output:**

| Metric                | Plain vLLM baseline |           Optimized |
| --------------------- | ------------------: | ------------------: |
| Total throughput      |      5,481.90 tok/s |     17,525.76 tok/s |
| Output throughput     |        608.31 tok/s |      1,944.77 tok/s |
| Mean TTFT             |             592.3 s |             143.1 s |
| P95 TTFT              | Approximately 800 s | Approximately 235 s |
| Mean TPOT             |           636.24 ms |           217.89 ms |
| Mean total completion |             651.5 s |             223.1 s |
| P95 total completion  |             857.9 s |             327.3 s |

The throughput improvement is 219.7%, corresponding to a theoretical 68.7% reduction in GPU cost per mixed token if all other costs and workload behavior remain constant. ([GPU Stack][15])

**Memory/utilization:** `mem-fraction-static=0.85`; actual HBM, SM utilization, memory-bandwidth utilization, power, and CPU usage were not reported.

**Expected savings:** 40–65% on the benchmark’s intended long-context/high-concurrency traffic; 0% or negative on mismatched traffic.

**Difficulty/maturity:** Medium engineering effort; underlying features are upstream and production-capable, but configuration is workload-specific.

**Interactions:** DP attention, DeepEP mode, maximum running requests, chunked prefill, KV dtype, concurrency, context length, and request mix.

**Reproduction core:**

```bash
--tp-size 8
--dp-size 8
--enable-dp-attention
--moe-a2a-backend deepep
--mem-fraction-static 0.85
--max-running-requests 256
--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3
```

**Disconfirming evidence:** GPUStack explicitly reports that the same optimized configuration regresses on short ShareGPT-like traffic because DP-attention and DeepEP overhead are not amortized. Its ShareGPT baseline had materially higher output throughput and much lower TTFT than the long-context-optimized configuration. ([GPU Stack][15])

### 4.2 FP8 KV cache

**Quality category:** Lossy relative to BF16 KV; potentially acceptable if FP8 KV is explicitly part of the sold SKU.

**Applicability:** Exact FP8 weights on H200, particularly long-context and KV-bound high-concurrency workloads.

**Reported results:**

* Total throughput: 14,451.05 to 17,525.76 tok/s, **+21.28%**.
* Mean TPOT: 255.63 to 217.89 ms, **−14.76%**.
* GSM8K sanity check: 0.975 for BF16 KV and 0.980 for FP8 KV over 200 examples; this is insufficient evidence for long-context distribution preservation. ([GPU Stack][15])

**Memory:** Approximately halves the bytes per KV element relative to BF16, though total runtime memory does not halve.

**Expected savings:** 0–18%. The main gain may appear as higher feasible concurrency or context rather than direct kernel speed.

**Difficulty/maturity:** Low implementation difficulty; mature upstream support. Medium-to-high validation burden.

**Interactions:** Prefix caching, context length, HBM pressure, batch size, P/D disaggregation, and cache offload.

**Reproduction:** `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3`.

**Disconfirming evidence:** The public quality check is small and arithmetic-focused. Quantization error may accumulate over long contexts, and the performance gain appears primarily in KV-bound regimes.

### 4.3 MTP speculative decoding and IndexShare

**Quality category:**

* Target-verified MTP speculation: distribution-preserving, not bitwise.
* IndexShare architectural reuse: intended mathematical equivalence.
* Published highest-throughput GLM‑5.2 stacks generally use NVFP4, which is lossy relative to the FP8 SKU.

**Applicability:** GLM‑5.2 on Blackwell, especially long-context agent traces with high prefix reuse.

**Public results:**

* Baseten attributes part of its 280+ TPS API to MTP, but does not isolate the MTP increment. ([Baseten][9])
* SGLang’s public work reports approximately 6–11% high-concurrency gain and about 11% end-to-end improvement from the newer speculation path on tested configurations; IndexShare can make draft steps substantially cheaper at long context. ([LMSYS Org][18])

**TTFT/ITL/tail:** Complete isolated distributions were not published for the exact FP8 H200 SKU.

**Memory/utilization:** Public reports provide configuration parameters but not a complete before/after HBM and utilization accounting.

**Expected savings for exact FP8:** 0–12%.

**Difficulty/maturity:** Medium-high. Upstream support exists, but acceptance rate, graph capture, memory allocation, and model-specific draft behavior require tuning.

**Interactions:** Sampling parameters, reasoning mode, context length, batch size, cache hits, CUDA graphs, and target-verification overhead.

**Reproduction:** Pin the exact MTP implementation and compare identical seeds and target logits with speculation disabled and enabled.

**Disconfirming evidence:** Gains depend on draft acceptance. Real customer sampling settings, tool tokens, structured output, and heterogeneous sequence lengths can materially reduce acceptance.

### 4.4 Prefill/decode disaggregation

**Quality category:** Mathematically equivalent if KV transfer and engine configurations preserve the reference computation; MTP remains distribution-preserving. Published Baseten results use lossy NVFP4 weights.

**Applicability:** Large, sustained fleets with materially different prefill and decode demand, enough nodes to avoid fragmentation, and fast KV transfer.

**Reported results:**

* Baseten reports 2× TPS on observed workload shapes from disaggregation.
* The mechanism uses separate prefill and decode engines, conditional routing, and NIXL-based KV transfer. ([Baseten][9])
* A separate public vLLM deployment for GLM‑5.2 NVFP4 used 24×B300 in a 4-prefill/1-decode topology and reported mean TTFT at or below 2.5 seconds and TPOT at or below 20 ms over its tested range. ([vLLM][19])

**Memory/utilization:** Public configurations disclose high memory-utilization targets but not complete before/after productive utilization, network utilization, or all-in power.

**Expected exact-FP8 COGS savings:** 0–30%. It can be negative at low load because of capacity fragmentation and transfer overhead.

**Difficulty/maturity:** High. Production maturity is emerging but requires reliable queueing, KV transfer, topology management, and failure recovery.

**Interactions:** Prefix cache, P/D ratio, prompt length after cache hit, NIXL/network topology, TP degree, MTP, CUDA graphs, and admission control.

**Reproduction:** Compare aggregated and P/D configurations at identical aggregate GPU count, arrival traces, context/output distributions, cache-hit rates, and latency objectives.

**Disconfirming evidence:** P/D primarily improves SLO isolation and phase-specific sizing. It does not inherently reduce COGS when the fleet is too small, the phase ratio changes quickly, or decode workers sit idle.

## 6. What could become proprietary

1. **Cross-window economic scheduler.** Jointly optimize admission, completion-window assignment, preemption, batch formation, failure reserve, and contribution margin rather than merely maximizing tokens per second.

2. **Exactness-aware model registry.** Cryptographically bind checkpoint, tokenizer, context, precision, KV dtype, parser behavior, server image, and evaluation evidence to each sold SKU.

3. **Per-request phase cost ledger.** Attribute novel prefill, cached-prefix residency, cache miss, decode, retry, duplicate execution, interruption loss, and warm-tail cost.

4. **Trace-trained capacity controller.** Predict prefill/decode demand by window and provision separate reliable, interruptible, and off-peak pools.

5. **Interruption-safe execution.** Resume or retry long requests without duplicate customer billing, inconsistent tool state, or uncontrolled repeated prefill.

6. **Cache economics and placement.** Optimize eviction, placement, offload, and routing against expected future reuse rather than generic LRU.

7. **Real-time price floors.** Stop admitting work into a tier when current node price, queue state, predicted reuse, or decode demand makes the request loss-making.

8. **Fleet procurement and provider portfolio.** Durable wholesale access can be more economically important than another low-single-digit kernel improvement.

## 7. Three experiments Arcten can run within two weeks

### Experiment 1: Exact FP8 throughput, latency, quality, and COGS Pareto

**Infrastructure**

* 8×H200 and, if accessible, 8×B200.
* Exact immutable `zai-org/GLM-5.2-FP8` manifest.
* Original benchmark images: vLLM 0.24.0 and SGLang 0.5.14.
* Current pinned images: vLLM 0.26.0 and SGLang 0.5.16.

**Matrix**

```text
Input:       1K, 8K, 32K, 128K, 512K, 1M
Output:      128, 1K, 8K
Concurrency: 1, 8, 32, 128, 256, 512
KV dtype:    BF16, FP8
MTP:         off, on
Prefix hit:  0%, 25%, 75%, 95%
Configs:     TP-only, TP/DP, DeepEP, workload-specific profiles
```

**Record**

* TTFT, TPOT, ITL, total completion p50/p95/p99.
* Input and output TPS per node and per GPU.
* HBM, SM, memory-bandwidth, network, CPU, and power utilization.
* Startup, model-load, and compilation time.
* OOMs, crashes, retries, and quality divergence.
* Logit and output-distribution differences from the reference configuration.

**Decision criterion**

A tier is eligible for a public price only when all-in phase COGS is at most **70% of price** across its intended workload distribution and failure scenario. For Standard output, that means demonstrated COGS at or below **$1.75/M**, not merely $2.50/M.

### Experiment 2: Completion-window scheduler replay

Construct synthetic and partner-derived traces containing:

```text
low constant load
correlated bursts
daily peaks
sustained saturation
long-agent serial turns
short independent requests
spot interruptions
node loss during prefill
node loss during decode
cold model start
provider capacity withdrawal
```

Compare:

* Immediate FIFO.
* Earliest-deadline-first.
* Cost-aware batching.
* Admission control with reliable and interruptible pools.
* Dynamic price-floor rejection.
* One-minute, five-minute, and Flex queues.

Measure:

* Productive utilization.
* Completion-window attainment.
* Warm-tail hours.
* Retry and duplicate-compute percentage.
* Gross margin by token type and customer.
* Tail completion time.
* Reliable-capacity reserve required.

**Decision criterion**

Do not launch Priority or Standard unless:

* At least 95% of the intended workload meets the stated **average-objective policy**.
* Productive utilization exceeds 80% under sustained traffic.
* Failure and retry reserve remains below 10% of GPU execution cost.
* Correlated bursts do not force systematic negative-margin fallback.

### Experiment 3: Cache residency and P/D economics

Use a realistic coding-agent trace, such as:

```text
~80K-token stable prefix
~220 output tokens/turn
13 serial turns
cache-hit rates: 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 92%
inter-turn delay: 1 second, 1 minute, 10 minutes, 1 hour
```

Compare:

* No cache.
* Local HBM prefix cache.
* CPU or remote cache offload.
* Aggregated inference.
* P/D disaggregation.
* Reliable and preemptible workers.
* Single-tenant and isolated multi-tenant cache policies.

Measure:

* HBM-hours per million cached tokens.
* Successful reuses per prefix-hour.
* Transfer and rehydration latency.
* Eviction and miss rate.
* Duplicate storage.
* Cache loss after interruption.
* TTFT and total trajectory time.
* Quality under BF16 and FP8 KV.

**Decision criterion**

Offer $0.12 cached pricing only if it remains below 70% COGS at a realistic observed reuse distribution—not a synthetic 92% cache-hit benchmark.

## 8. Three-month engineering recommendation

### Weeks 1–2: Measurement and contracting

* Complete the three experiments.
* Obtain written H200 and B200 wholesale quotes.
* Establish an immutable exactness manifest.
* Obtain counsel-reviewed terms for every provider relationship.
* Do not depend on retail API resale.
* Exclude credits, free tiers, and launch promotions from the cost ledger.

### Weeks 3–6: Narrow MVP

Launch one exact FP8 model, one region, and one hardware profile.

Products:

* **Now:** private pilot at $1.40/$0.26/$4.40, conditioned on minimum volume or prepaid commitment.
* **Standard:** asynchronous design-partner pilot, initially priced above the proposed $2.50 output rate.
* No fixed-price Flex tier yet.

Build:

* OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
* Immutable model manifest.
* Per-request phase cost accounting.
* Admission and price-floor control.
* Prefix-cache isolation.
* Queue-aware routing.
* Explicit cancellation, timeout, and billing semantics.

Use upstream vLLM/SGLang. Do not start custom CUDA work until profiling identifies a stable, material cost center that upstream does not already address.

### Weeks 7–10: Queue and failure system

* Add one reliable pool and one interruptible pool.
* Implement interruption-aware retry and customer-billing deduplication.
* Add workload-specific serving profiles rather than one universal configuration.
* Run fault injection, burst, cold-start, and provider-withdrawal tests.
* Express one- and five-minute products as average objectives with disclosed variability, not strict per-request deadlines.

### Weeks 11–12: Design-partner production trial

Use two or three customers with:

* Minimum monthly commitments.
* Known input/output distributions.
* Explicit context and output caps.
* Cache-isolation agreements.
* Average completion objectives.
* Temporary price floors or workload-specific quotes.

### Recommended initial pricing posture

| Tier         | Recommendation                                                                                            |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Now**      | Keep $1.40/$0.26/$4.40 only with wholesale/owner-equivalent economics and minimum volume                  |
| **Priority** | Pilot $0.70/$0.18/$3.00; require workload qualification and commitment                                    |

A public Standard or Flex commitment should require:

```text
all-in COGS ≤ 70% of rate
under sustained representative traces
including warm tail, retries, interruptions,
orchestration, networking, payment overhead,
and customer-specific cache behavior
```

## 9. Explicit reasons the approach might fail

1. **Insufficient aggregate demand.** Waiting helps only when enough compatible work accumulates to form efficient batches.

2. **Correlated traffic.** Customer bursts may coincide rather than smooth one another.

3. **Decode remains sequential.** Queueing improves utilization but does not remove the per-output-token memory and synchronization cost.

4. **Output mix is worse than forecast.** Reasoning and coding agents can generate far more output than initial pricing models assume.

5. **Warm-tail cost dominates.** Low-volume model SKUs may require an eight-GPU warm replica whose idle cost overwhelms token revenue.

6. **Spot savings disappear in retries.** Long prefill, compilation, model loading, KV loss, and repeated decode can consume the nominal spot discount.

7. **Reliable fallback is expensive.** A one- or five-minute commitment can require reserved capacity even when most work runs on cheaper capacity.

8. **Strict deadlines destroy the economic benefit.** Sail’s public windows are average targets; a hard deadline requires substantially more reserve.

9. **Cache reuse is overestimated.** Prefixes may differ because of timestamps, tool schemas, retrieved documents, message ordering, or tokenizer changes.

10. **Cache residency is underpriced.** Large long-lived prefixes consume HBM even while no tokens are executing.

11. **Privacy prevents pooling.** Cross-tenant cache reuse may be technically, contractually, or reputationally unacceptable.

12. **FP8 KV changes quality.** Small benchmark parity does not prove long-context or tool-call distribution preservation.

13. **MTP acceptance is low on real traffic.** Tool tokens, JSON constraints, unusual temperatures, and diverse reasoning traces can reduce speculative gains.

14. **P/D fragments a small fleet.** Separate prefill and decode workers can sit idle and increase cost.

15. **Network or KV transfer becomes the bottleneck.** Disaggregation can move the constraint rather than remove it.

16. **The optimal configuration regresses common traffic.** GPUStack’s long-context configuration already demonstrates regression on short ShareGPT-like requests.

17. **Provider interruption is correlated.** Spot or discounted capacity may disappear across regions and suppliers during the same market event.

18. **Procurement disadvantage.** Established providers may have hardware ownership, long-term commitments, or strategic contracts unavailable to Arcten.

19. **Promotional pricing anchors customer expectations.** Competitors may temporarily sell below sustainable COGS.

20. **First-party strategic pricing.** Z.AI or a hyperscaler can rationally price below a standalone inference company’s required margin.

21. **Retail resale rights are unavailable.** A technically viable upstream route may not be legally resellable under Arcten’s branding.

22. **Checkpoint drift breaks exactness.** Silent model, tokenizer, chat-template, kernel, parser, or KV-dtype changes can invalidate the sold SKU.

23. **Customer workloads evade batching.** Incompatible sampling parameters, structured outputs, adapters, context lengths, and latency needs reduce batch compatibility.

24. **Failure accounting is incomplete.** Duplicate prefill, abandoned generations, payment failures, abuse, support, and egress can erase a thin phase margin.

25. **B200 gains do not transfer to exact GLM‑5.2 FP8.** Adjacent-model or NVFP4 benchmark gains may not reproduce on the required checkpoint.

## Final recommendation

Arcten should proceed with the product thesis, but not with the full proposed price curve.

The defensible near-term product is:

* One hash-attested FP8 model.
* Now plus an asynchronous Standard pilot.
* Owner-equivalent or negotiated wholesale capacity.
* Average completion objectives.
* Workload-qualified pricing.
* A dynamic economic scheduler and phase-level cost ledger.
* Gateway BYOK routing where Arcten cannot economically or contractually host the requested route.

The defensible public benchmark is approximately **$1.40 input / $4.40 output**. Approximately **$3.00 output** is a thin owner-scale target. **$2.50** is currently a technical milestone rather than a sustainable list price, and **$1.80** should be treated as an unproven B200/off-peak objective.
