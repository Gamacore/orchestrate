## Executive conclusion

**Provisional GLM launch choice:** SGLang 0.5.16, with vLLM 0.26.0 as a mandatory same-node challenger and fallback. This is not a measured winner declaration. The current SGLang release contains more GLM-5.2-specific work, while both engines have documented paths for the exact model. ([Hugging Face][1])

Recommended baseline:

```text
Model:      zai-org/GLM-5.2-FP8@ba978f7
Hardware:   8×B200 180GB
Parallel:   TP8
Context:    native 1,048,576
Weights:    exact requested FP8 files
KV cache:   BF16
Caching:    disabled during qualification
MTP/spec:   disabled during qualification
Offload:    disabled
```

TensorRT-LLM 1.3.0rc23 and LMDeploy 0.15.0 should remain **qualification-only** for GLM. Their current documentation establishes generic GLM-5-family coverage, not the complete exact combination of `GLM-5.2-FP8`, BF16 KV, native one-million-token context, tokenizer/template parity, and stable streaming. TensorRT-LLM is also currently an RC release. ([GitHub][2])

**One-GPU comparison model:** `Qwen/Qwen3-32B-FP8@aa55da1`, using one H100 80GB, TP1, native 32,768-token context, and BF16 KV. The official artifact is approximately 34.3GB and represents a materially fairer four-engine qualification lane than using an unsupported or converted model. ([Hugging Face][3])

## Pricing conclusion

At 70% productive utilization and a provisional 15% non-GPU overhead reserve, an 8×B200 node must sustain approximately:

| Target                         | Lambda-like on-demand | CoreWeave-like on-demand |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------: | -----------------------: |
| Flex output, zero gross margin |          13,569 tok/s |             17,443 tok/s |
| Flex output, 40% gross margin  |      **22,615 tok/s** |         **29,071 tok/s** |

These are formula-derived whole-node gates based on current public rates, not engine-performance estimates. They exclude the possibility that actual storage, orchestration, failures, payment costs, or low utilization exceed the 15% placeholder. ([CoreWeave][4])

Therefore:

* **Do not commit to Flex at $1.80/M output.**
* Treat Priority and Standard as private-alpha experiments.
* Isolate Now traffic from delayed traffic or reserve hard headroom.
* Require measured full COGS and deadline goodput before publishing binding completion windows.
* Waiting creates scheduling and capacity optionality; it does not reduce the compute required by an unchanged request.

## What was executed

The package passed:

* 10 unit tests.
* 24/24 deterministic mock streaming requests.
* Exact mock output self-comparison.
* Python compilation.
* YAML, JSON, and CSV validation.
* Shell syntax validation.
* Report/source-reference checks.
* Cost-model regression checks.
* ZIP integrity verification.

**No NVIDIA GPU was exposed in the execution environment.** Consequently, SGLang, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, and LMDeploy throughput, TTFT, TPOT, p99 latency, HBM use, utilization, power, long-context behavior, and fault recovery remain explicitly `NOT_MEASURED`. No unrelated public benchmark was substituted.

## Recommended two-week experiments

1. **Exact-support gate:** hash both immutable snapshots; qualify SGLang/vLLM on GLM and all four engines on Qwen; test native context, BF16 KV, streaming, cancellation, token accounting, and deterministic correctness. Record unsupported configurations rather than converting them.

2. **Performance and full-COGS envelope:** execute batch-one, saturation, mixed-length, long-context, prefix-sharing, and burst tests with three randomized repetitions and equal tuning budgets. Select by cost per SLO-compliant token, not peak token throughput.

3. **Delayed-window scheduler test:** compare immediate FIFO, isolated pools, and deadline/cache-aware scheduling under low load, sustained load, bursts, worker loss, restart, network degradation, and spot interruption.

## Three-month recommendation

Build around replaceable SGLang/vLLM backends rather than maintaining a permanent engine fork. The potentially proprietary layer is:

* deadline-aware admission and scheduling;
* tenant-safe cache-affinity routing;
* phase-aware marginal cost estimation;
* interruption-safe retries and duplicate suppression;
* capacity selection across on-demand and interruptible supply;
* continuous exact-model regression and autotuning.

Continuous batching, paged KV memory, FlashAttention/FlashInfer, prefix caching, CUDA graphs, chunked prefill, MTP primitives, multi-GPU parallelism, and basic OpenAI-compatible serving are already commodity capabilities.

The principal risk is that compatible delayed volume, cache reuse, utilization improvement, and interruptible-capacity savings are insufficient to overcome decode cost, warm-tail capacity, retries, failure reserve, and customer tail-latency requirements.
