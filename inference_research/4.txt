**Research date:** August 1, 2026. Prices are public list prices before negotiated discounts, taxes, credits, and Arcten’s non-GPU costs. “Available” below means publicly catalogued unless explicitly described as guaranteed; no public page proves that an exact 8-GPU node is allocatable at this instant.

### Main conclusions

1. **Do not make the proposed price card binding yet.** Under a plausible sustained H200 scenario, Arcten must sustain approximately **2,835 aggregate output tokens/s per 8×H200 node merely to break even** at the $4.40/M Now output price, and about **3,780 tokens/s for a 25% gross margin**. At low load, the required throughput becomes approximately 16,500 tokens/s, illustrating that utilization is more important than the lowest catalog rate.

2. **The $1.40 input / $0.26 cached / $4.40 output “Now” target is already the public GLM‑5.2 list price at both Together and Crusoe.** This establishes a market reference, not Arcten’s achievable COGS. Neither public price page establishes identical checkpoint hashes, tokenizer hashes, context limits, KV-cache precision, or serving behavior. ([Together AI][1])

3. **Exact GLM‑5.2 FP8 is an 8-GPU node procurement problem.** The official model card describes a roughly 753B-parameter FP8 checkpoint and 1M context. The official vLLM recipe places single-node FP8 serving on 8×H200-class hardware but uses 8×B200 with FP8 KV cache for full 1M context. An H200 service with a lower context cap and a B200 service with full 1M context are not equivalent products. ([Hugging Face][2])

4. **The cheapest credible Flex capacity is Verda spot**, but it can be reclaimed without warning, including shortly after creation. Its raw H200 price is 67.4% below Crusoe H200 on-demand, but a scenario-based effective saving is much wider—approximately **16% to 73%, central case 52%**—after utilization, retries, cache destruction, and ancillary costs. This is a planning range, not a statistical confidence interval. ([Verda][3])

5. **A multi-provider fleet is justified for option value and reliability, not automatically for lower COGS.** The recommended topology is:

   * one warm, non-preemptible primary;
   * one interruptible overflow provider;
   * one cold or scheduled break-glass provider.

   Running duplicate warm fleets across several providers at low load will increase COGS and fragment batching and prefix caches.

## 2. Exact service boundary and quality classification

| Dimension                                         | Required classification                                      | Implication                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Checkpoint and tokenizer artifacts                | **Bitwise identity**                                         | Pin model revision SHA, every shard checksum, tokenizer files, chat template, generation configuration, and container digest.   |
| H200 versus B200 execution                        | Intended **mathematical equivalence**, not bitwise identity  | Different GPUs, kernels, reduction order, batching, and CUDA/NCCL versions can change floating-point results.                   |
| Stochastic outputs across providers               | Must demonstrate **distribution preservation**               | Compare token distributions and repeated-sample behavior; matching model names are insufficient.                                |
| FP8 KV cache versus BF16 KV cache                 | **Lossy approximation**                                      | KV-cache dtype must be part of the advertised service specification. Full 1M serving in the referenced B200 recipe uses FP8 KV. |
| NVFP4, MXFP4, INT4, AWQ, GPTQ, or similar weights | **Lossy approximation**                                      | Excluded from comparison with the FP8 checkpoint.                                                                               |
| H200 capped context versus B200 1M context        | Different service specification                              | It violates the brief to present the capped H200 deployment as equivalent to full-context B200 service.                         |
| Spot retry on a deterministic request             | Same artifacts, but not necessarily bitwise-identical output | A retry can change stochastic output unless seed and execution conditions are controlled.                                       |
| Procurement, queueing, and request aggregation    | No inherent model-quality change                             | They affect latency, availability, and completion distribution, not weight identity.                                            |

The official deployment guidance currently calls for vLLM 0.23.0 or newer or SGLang 0.5.13.post1 or newer. These versions should be pinned for testing rather than treated as open-ended minimums. ([vLLM Recipes][4])

## 4. Reproducible calculations

### 4.1 Cost model

For each node:

[
E =
\frac{
(r_{\text{GPU}} + r_{\text{CPU/RAM}} + r_{\text{storage}} +
r_{\text{network}} + r_{\text{orchestration}} +
r_{\text{provider/payment}})
\times f_{\text{retry}}
}{
u_{\text{productive}}
}
]

Where:

* (E) is dollars per productive node-hour.
* (u_{\text{productive}}) is token-processing time divided by billed GPU time. It therefore captures model loading, compilation, idle time, warm tails, and queue gaps.
* (f_{\text{retry}}) captures interruption, failure, duplicate work, and replay.
* Credits are zero.

Then:

[
\text{Input COGS/M} =
\frac{E \times 10^6}{T_{\text{prefill}}\times 3600}
]

[
\text{Output COGS/M} =
\frac{E \times 10^6}{T_{\text{decode}}\times 3600}
]

[
\text{Cached-input COGS/M} =
b_{\text{rebuild}}\times \text{Input COGS/M} +
c_{\text{KV retention, lookup, replication}}
]

There is no defensible fixed cached-input saving without measuring cache reuse, eviction, HBM occupation, rebuilds following interruption, and cross-replica duplication.

### Multi-provider break-even example

Suppose Arcten keeps a separate Crusoe H200 backup warm at **$34.32/hour** and shifts flexible demand from Verda OD at $32/hour to Verda spot at $11.20/hour.

[
\text{Savings per shifted node-hour} = 32 - 11.20 = 20.80
]

[
\text{Break-even shifted load} =
\frac{34.32}{20.80} = 1.65
]

Arcten must average more than **1.65 spot nodes of shifted work for every continuously warm backup node** before the raw arbitrage funds that backup. Queue fragmentation, cache loss, and orchestration raise the threshold. At pre-product load, active-active warm duplication is unlikely to beat one primary provider.

## 6. What is already commodity

* Continuous batching, paged KV allocation, prefix caching, FlashAttention/FlashInfer-class kernels, CUDA graphs, tensor parallelism, and common vLLM/SGLang deployment.
* Kubernetes or Slurm fleet provisioning, health checks, retries, and ordinary autoscaling.
* S3-compatible model mirroring and immutable container images.
* Basic spot interruption handling and provider-price comparison.
* Metrics for TTFT, inter-token latency, token throughput, GPU utilization, and memory.
* Routing based on static model name, provider availability, or list price.

A product whose principal advantage is merely “we use SGLang, batching, and cheaper spot GPUs” is not defensible.

## 7. What could become proprietary

1. **Completion-window-aware admission control.** Predict whether a request can be delayed and consolidated without missing its promised completion distribution.

2. **Marginal prefill/decode COGS scheduling.** Route based on current batch composition, prefix reuse, expected output length, HBM occupancy, decode saturation, and interruption hazard—not average node price.

3. **Empirical capacity-risk models.** Maintain provider/SKU/region-specific distributions for acquisition time, reclamation risk, failure rate, model-ready time, and recovery cost.

4. **Cache-aware multi-provider placement.** Keep compatible prefixes together and account for the cost of moving or rebuilding caches before chasing a cheaper GPU.

5. **Exact-artifact attestation.** Record checkpoint SHA, tokenizer SHA, container digest, CUDA/NCCL/engine versions, KV dtype, context cap, kernel choices, and generation parameters for every deployment.

6. **Workload-specific prefill/decode separation.** Potentially place prefill and decode on different capacity pools—but only if measured transfer and coordination costs produce a real net saving.

## 8. Three experiments Arcten can run within two weeks

### Experiment 1 — Exact-model performance and quality qualification

**Providers:** Verda, Crusoe, and Nebius 8×H200. Add Verda or Lambda 8×B200 for the full-context configuration.

**Pinned configuration**

* Exact `zai-org/GLM-5.2-FP8` revision and shard checksums.
* Exact tokenizer and chat-template hashes.
* One immutable container digest.
* Fixed CUDA, NCCL, driver, vLLM/SGLang, FlashInfer, and kernel versions.
* Explicit KV dtype.
* Identical context cap and generation parameters.

**Workload matrix**

* Input lengths: 1k, 8k, 32k, 128k; add 1M-class test only on the B200 full-context service.
* Output lengths: 256, 2k, and 8k.
* Arrival patterns: low-load Poisson, sharp burst, sustained saturation, and mixed-length production trace.
* Cold, warm-model, cold-prefix, and warm-prefix states.

**Record**

* TTFT, inter-token latency, total completion time, and p50/p95/p99.
* Aggregate prefill and decode tokens/s.
* GPU utilization, HBM use, power where exposed, queue depth, and batch composition.
* Model download, load, graph-capture, and ready time.
* Failed requests, NCCL errors, and restart time.
* Effective COGS per million input, cached, and output tokens.

**Quality tests**

* Greedy exact-output comparison.
* Per-token logit/top-k divergence on a fixed corpus.
* Repeated stochastic generations and distributional comparison.
* Long-context retrieval and generation evaluations.
* Separate report for FP8 versus BF16 KV if both are tested.

**Pass gate:** the measured p95 workload mix must achieve no more than 75% of the proposed retail price in complete COGS, including warm tail and failures. For the modeled sustained Now case, output throughput should be at least approximately **3,800 aggregate tokens/s**.

### Experiment 2 — Spot interruption and recovery economics

**Providers:** Verda spot, Nebius preemptible, and CoreWeave spot.

Run both natural and injected node failures while serving queued Flex traffic.

Measure:

* Actual notice duration.
* Completed work lost at interruption.
* Duplicate token computation.
* Model and prefix-cache reload time.
* Cache-hit-rate degradation after replacement.
* Retry count and additional GPU-hours.
* Allocation success and replacement-node acquisition time.
* Completion-window miss distribution.
* Difference between checkpoint/restart, whole-request replay, and speculative duplicate execution.

Produce an empirical:

[
f_{\text{retry}} =
\frac{\text{total GPU work including retries}}
{\text{useful completed GPU work}}
]

**Pass gate:** measured effective Flex COGS must support $1.80/M output with the desired margin. Under the planning scenario, this means approximately **4,400 aggregate output tokens/s** on Verda spot for 25% gross margin. Spot should not be used for a strict one-minute promise unless the request is already running on secured capacity.

### Experiment 3 — Live capacity and contracting probe

For seven days:

* Poll exact H200/B200 SKU inventory at five-minute intervals where APIs permit.
* Perform a controlled number of real launches across target regions and times of day.
* Record allocation success, allocation latency, VM-ready time, model-ready time, and unexplained failures.
* Inspect whether eight GPUs are one contiguous NVSwitch/SXM node.
* Query AWS Capacity Block and GCP Calendar inventory for representative future windows.
* Obtain written quotations for 30-, 90-, and 180-day capacity.
* Obtain written confirmation that Arcten may operate a commercial hosted-inference API on the rented infrastructure.
* Obtain explicit language covering customer data, subprocessors, model artifacts, indemnity, termination, preemption, credits, and data deletion.

**Pass gate:** Now capacity must be warm or contractually acquired; public OD catalog availability is not an acceptable Now capacity guarantee.

## 9. Three-month engineering recommendation

### Month 1 — Establish ground truth

* Complete the three experiments.
* Build immutable artifact manifests and automatic deployment verification.
* Separate an **H200 capped-context SKU** from a **B200 full-context SKU**.
* Mirror weights in at least two providers, but do not keep two complete fleets warm.
* Model prefill, decode, cached-input, warm-tail, and retry COGS separately.
* Do not publish binding prices or per-request timing guarantees.

### Month 2 — Private beta

* Select one H200 primary based on measured economics and contract terms, not catalog price alone.
* Likely finalists: Crusoe, Nebius, or Verda after legal clearance.
* Use Verda or Nebius interruptible capacity only for Flex overflow.
* Implement queue-aware admission control and reject or defer requests when the promised window cannot be met.
* Expose the exact model revision, context cap, KV precision, and serving configuration.
* Launch B200 full-context support only as a distinct SKU with distinct pricing.

### Month 3 — Limited multi-provider production

* Operate one primary provider and one overflow provider.
* Configure a third provider as cold or scheduled break-glass capacity.
* Add provider-specific acquisition and interruption models.
* Route using marginal COGS, queue state, cache locality, and deadline risk.
* Commit for 90–180 days only after measured base demand can keep the committed node pool above roughly 70% productive utilization.
* Do not sign a one-year GPU commitment during the pre-product period.

### Commercial gates before binding the proposed prices

1. Complete COGS at the p95 workload mix is at most 75% of retail revenue.
2. Sustained Now output throughput reaches approximately 3,800 aggregate tokens/s per modeled H200 node, or the price is raised.
3. Flex output reaches approximately 4,400 tokens/s under measured Verda-like spot economics, or the $1.80 price is raised.
4. Actual interruption and recovery data support the advertised completion distribution.
5. Every provider contract explicitly permits commercial hosted inference.
6. Checkpoint, tokenizer, context, weight precision, KV precision, and serving configuration are disclosed and verified.
7. Low-load COGS is covered by minimum spend, queueing, higher Now prices, or another demand-shaping mechanism.

## 10. Explicit reasons the approach might fail

1. **Decode throughput may be too low.** At 2,000 aggregate output tokens/s, modeled sustained Now COGS is about $6.24/M, above the proposed $4.40 price.

2. **Low demand destroys the economics.** One warm H200 node at 15% productive utilization produces modeled output COGS of more than $36/M even at 2,000 decode tokens/s.

3. **Spot savings may disappear after interruption.** Repeated model loading, retrying long outputs, losing prefix caches, and waiting for replacement capacity can consume most of the catalog discount.

4. **Compatible requests may not arrive together.** Differences in model revision, adapter, context length, decoding parameters, latency tolerance, and prefix content reduce the batching pool.

5. **Long completion windows do not guarantee high utilization.** A request can wait five minutes without enough compatible work arriving to form an economically superior batch.

6. **Full-context traffic can create adverse selection.** Customers most attracted to inexpensive 1M-context service may generate the largest KV-memory and prefill burden while paying the lowest delayed-input price.

7. **The cheapest catalog capacity may not be allocatable.** Marketplace listings and per-GPU “from” prices do not prove one contiguous 8-GPU NVSwitch node is available.

8. **Commercial terms may block the fleet design.** Vast publicly prohibits service-bureau use; Verda’s public restrictions require clarification; other providers may require an OEM or managed-service agreement.

9. **Multi-provider operation can reduce, rather than improve, utilization.** Splitting queues, replicas, and prefixes across providers can lower batch density and cache hit rates.

10. **Hardware and kernel differences prevent bitwise identity.** Even with identical weights and tokenizer, H200/B200, CUDA versions, reductions, and batching can change floating-point results.

11. **The proposed Now price is not differentiated.** Together and Crusoe already publicly list the same GLM‑5.2 token price, potentially with greater fleet scale and purchasing leverage. ([Together AI][1])

12. **Serverless cold-start behavior may be incompatible with short windows.** Provider infrastructure launch time excludes loading and initializing the roughly 0.8 TiB GLM‑5.2 artifact.

13. **Output-heavy workloads can erase input-side savings.** Prefill targets appear materially easier than decode targets; a customer mix with long generations can make the blended price unprofitable.

14. **Commitment can become stranded capacity.** The best public reserved H200 rate requires a 91–180-day commitment before Arcten has validated stable base demand.

**Recommendation:** proceed with experiments, not customer commitments. Build the first private beta around a capped-context H200 service, use spot only for Flex, and treat full-1M B200 service as a separately priced product.
