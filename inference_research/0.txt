# GLM-5.2 FP8 inference economics

**Assessment date: August 1, 2026**

## 1. Executive conclusion

**Do not make the four proposed prices binding yet.**

The economic thesis is conditionally valid: waiting reduces cost only when the delay permits Arcten to use cheaper capacity, raise productive utilization, improve cache locality, or schedule around bursts. A longer completion window does not itself reduce GPU work.

The proposed **Now** price exactly matches Z.ai’s retail GLM-5.2 price. Consequently, retail API resale at that price has no gross margin before payment, support, and retry costs. Fireworks also lists GLM-5.2 at the same uncached-input and output prices, while its batch service gives a 50% discount at much longer completion windows. These provider listings do not establish that they expose the exact FP8 checkpoint, revision, tokenizer, context limit, or KV precision required by Arcten. ([Overview - Z.AI DEVELOPER DOCUMENT][1])

### Tier verdict

The thresholds below use one CoreWeave 8×B200 node at $68.80/hour, 85% productive utilization, and a 10% retry/failure reserve. “Prefill” and “decode” are separate phase goodputs, not simultaneous headline throughput. The 30% column also deducts a 2.9% variable payment fee but still excludes fixed payment fees, control-plane costs, support, and cold starts. CoreWeave’s node includes 1,440 GB aggregate HBM, 128 vCPUs, 2,048 GB RAM, and 61.44 TB local storage. ([CoreWeave][2])

| Tier                | Zero-margin minimum prefill / decode | Minimum for 30% margin | Conclusion                                                                                                        |
| ------------------- | -----------------------------------: | ---------------------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Now**             |                  17.7k / 5.62k tok/s |    26.3k / 8.38k tok/s | Technically plausible at sustained utilization. No price differentiation from Z.ai retail.                        |
| **Priority**        |                        35.3k / 8.24k |          52.7k / 12.3k | Plausible only with strong exact-FP8 performance or discounted compute. Proposed margin is thin.                  |
| **Standard**        |                        49.5k / 9.89k |          73.7k / 14.7k | Not currently supported by public exact-FP8 evidence. Approximately break-even in the planning model.             |
| **Flex, on-demand** |                        61.8k / 13.7k |          92.1k / 20.5k | Not sustainable at list-price on-demand compute.                                                                  |
| **Flex, spot**      |                        37.9k / 8.42k |          56.5k / 12.6k | Potentially viable, but only with high utilization, low interruption waste, and probabilistic completion targets. |

No public benchmark demonstrates those rates for the exact `GLM-5.2-FP8` checkpoint. The strongest production result is for **GLM-5.2-NVFP4**, which is a lossy, different-precision model and cannot validate FP8 COGS. The public vLLM GLM-5.2 optimization tracker also shows that important block-FP8 decode, FP8 KV, communication, and Blackwell optimizations are still active work. ([vLLM][3])

**Recommended decision:** validate **Now plus one experimental queued tier**, probably Priority. Do not publish Standard or Flex at the proposed prices until exact-FP8 measurements clear the 30%-margin thresholds under representative traffic and failure conditions.

## 2. Exact serving contract

The current `zai-org/GLM-5.2-FP8` repository is approximately 756 GB across 141 weight shards. The current revision should be pinned to the full commit `ba978f7d347eaf65d22f1a86833408afdb953541`, rather than tracking `main`. The model has a 1,048,576-token configured context window, 78 layers, 256 routed experts, eight selected experts per token, `kv_lora_rank=512`, and an FP8 E4M3 block quantization configuration. ([Hugging Face][4])

It should not be described as a graph that is entirely FP8. The checkpoint uses block-FP8 quantized modules while retaining a BF16 base dtype and explicit modules that are not converted. The contract must identify both the checkpoint format and those higher-precision components. ([Hugging Face][5])

The official vLLM recipe states that the checkpoint can run on 8×H200 or 8×H20, while the full 1M-token context requires 8×B200. Its reference FP8 launch uses tensor parallelism of eight, FP8 KV cache, and five-token MTP speculation. ([vLLM Recipes][6])

### Required identity manifest

Every Arcten deployment should record:

* Full model repository revision and SHA-256 of all 141 weight shards.
* Hashes for `config.json`, tokenizer files, special-token files, generation configuration, and chat template.
* Maximum context: exactly 1,048,576 tokens unless the customer requests a smaller operational limit.
* Weight quantization: native E4M3 block-FP8 checkpoint, including module exclusions.
* KV-cache precision: explicitly FP8 E4M3 or another declared value; it is not implied by weight precision.
* Exact vLLM/SGLang commit, container digest, Transformers version, CUDA, driver, NCCL, FlashInfer/FlashAttention, and kernel configuration.
* Sampling parameters, seed, logit processors, structured-output implementation, and tool/reasoning parsers.

### Quality categories

| Category                      | Meaning for Arcten                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bitwise identity**          | Same model and runtime manifest, deterministic kernels, fixed batch shape/order, fixed hardware, and fixed seed produce identical token IDs or logits. Dynamic production batching will usually prevent this guarantee. |
| **Mathematical equivalence**  | Paged KV, continuous batching, exact prefix-cache reuse, CUDA graphs, kernel fusion, TP/EP, and P/D disaggregation compute the same mathematical operation, subject to floating-point reordering.                       |
| **Distribution preservation** | Speculative decoding or MTP preserves the target sampling distribution only when draft verification and acceptance/rejection are correct for the configured sampler. This must be tested, not assumed.                  |
| **Lossy approximation**       | NVFP4, MXFP4, INT4, approximate KV compression, changed sparse-attention/index-sharing patterns, or model substitution. These must not be sold as equivalent to the requested FP8 model.                                |

## 4. Reproducible calculations

### Core equations

Let:

* (R) = total node and infrastructure dollars per hour.
* (U) = productive utilization, excluding idle and warm-tail time.
* (r) = interruption, retry, and failure reserve.
* (P) = uncached-prefill goodput in tokens/second.
* (D) = generated-token goodput in tokens/second.

[
R_{\text{effective}} =
\frac{
R_{\text{GPU+CPU+RAM}}
+R_{\text{storage}}
+R_{\text{network}}
+R_{\text{orchestration}}
}{
U
}
(1+r)
]

[
COGS_{\text{input}} =
\frac{R_{\text{effective}}\times10^6}{3600P}
]

[
COGS_{\text{output}} =
\frac{R_{\text{effective}}\times10^6}{3600D}
]

[
COGS_{\text{cold}}/M =
\frac{R_{\text{node}}\times\text{cold-start hours}\times10^6}
{\text{productive tokens before next cold start}}
]

For a request with uncached input (I_u), cached input (I_c), and output (O):

[
COGS_{\text{request}} =
I_u C_{\text{input}}+
I_c C_{\text{cached}}+
O C_{\text{output}}+
C_{\text{cold}}+
C_{\text{payment}}
]

When prefill and decode use separate pools, each pool needs its own rate, utilization, failure reserve, and phase goodput. KV-transfer CPU and network costs must then be added. Do not insert simultaneous whole-system input/output throughput into both phase formulas; that double-counts capacity.

Consequences:

* **Low load:** no tier is viable. The 756 GB model cannot be kept warm for sparse demand without minimum commitments or cross-customer aggregation.
* **Burst:** even Now is output-negative at the assumed service rate.
* **Sustained:** Standard input is already below cost and output has only about a 1% GPU margin before other expenses.
* **Flex/spot:** Flex has only about a 16% component margin before payment, cold starts, orchestration, and support.
* **Failure/fallback:** output COGS exceeds even Now pricing, showing why spot savings cannot be treated as fixed.

### Price floor at the sustained assumptions

Using a 30% gross-margin bar and a 2.9% variable payment fee—but still excluding cold starts, control plane and fixed fees—the approximate minimum sustainable prices are:

| Infrastructure scenario                    |       Input | Cached input |      Output |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------: | -----------: | ----------: |
| Sustained on-demand, with two cache reuses | **$0.82/M** |  **$0.13/M** | **$3.69/M** |
| Flex spot, with two cache reuses           | **$0.50/M** |  **$0.07/M** | **$2.26/M** |

Therefore:

* The proposed Priority price of **$0.70/$0.18/$3.00** misses the input and output margin floor under the sustained planning assumptions.
* Standard is materially below it.
* Flex is below even the spot-based input and output floor.
* Actual required prices would be higher after orchestration, support, cold-start amortization and fixed payment costs.

### Cached-input economics

From the model configuration, a lower-bound FP8 MLA KV payload for one 1M-token prefix is:

[
(512+64)\times78\times1\text{ byte/token}
=44{,}928\text{ bytes/token}
]

That is approximately **44.9 GB per 1M-token prefix**, excluding allocator overhead, fragmentation, indexer state, MTP buffers and runtime workspace. Fifteen such prefixes would consume approximately 674 GB; combined with the 756 GB checkpoint, that nearly exhausts a B200 node’s 1,440 GB HBM before runtime overhead. ([Hugging Face][4])

A proportional HBM opportunity-cost calculation for retaining one such prefix for five minutes gives:

| Capacity       | Five-minute HBM opportunity cost | Cached hits required to cover price                   |
| -------------- | -------------------------------: | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| B200 on-demand |                           $0.179 | Now: 0.69; Priority: 0.99; Standard: 1.49; Flex: 2.24 |
| B200 spot      |                           $0.089 | Flex: 1.11                                            |

Operational interpretation:

* Standard needs approximately **two paid cache hits** within five minutes.
* Flex needs approximately **three on-demand hits** or **two spot hits**.
* This is only an opportunity-cost heuristic. It overstates incremental cost when a node would otherwise be idle and understates it when HBM pressure forces another node or reduces batching capacity.
* CPU-tier caching can reduce HBM pressure but adds transfer latency, bandwidth demand, and failure modes.

### Loading, compilation, storage, and payments

The GLM-5 SGLang compile report provides a 15–30 minute B200 proxy. At $68.80/hour, that is **$17.20–$34.40** of node time per compilation event. Repeating it every 50M productive tokens adds $0.344–$0.688/M; every 500M tokens adds $0.034–$0.069/M. The exact GLM-5.2 model load and compilation time must be measured independently. ([GitHub][10])

CoreWeave hot object storage is $0.06/GB/month, making one 756 GB copy approximately $45/month; distributed file storage is approximately $53/month. Transfer within CoreWeave and to/from the internet is listed as free. Storage dollars are minor relative to GPU time, but transferring and materializing 756 GB during recovery can make the one- and five-minute windows impossible. ([CoreWeave][2])

Stripe’s illustrative domestic-card rate is 2.9% plus $0.30. Arcten should bill through account top-ups or consolidated invoices; applying a $0.30 fee to individual API requests would dominate request economics. ([Stripe][13])

Spot interruption notices cannot be treated as an SLA. AWS notices are two minutes and best-effort; GCP’s default is a 30-second best-effort shutdown period; Azure provides approximately 30 seconds and guarantees no minimum run time. Retry waste and warm fallback capacity must therefore be measured as costs, not treated as edge cases. ([AWS Documentation][14])

## 5. What is already commodity

As of August 1, 2026, current stable releases include vLLM 0.25.1, SGLang 0.5.16, and Transformers 5.14.1. These should be pinned by commit or container digest rather than installed as floating “latest” dependencies. ([GitHub][15])

The following are not defensible moats by themselves:

| Commodity capability                     | Current baseline                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| OpenAI-compatible serving                | Included in vLLM and SGLang                                        |
| Continuous batching and chunked prefill  | Standard engine features                                           |
| Paged KV memory                          | Standard vLLM capability                                           |
| Prefix caching                           | Standard; each DP rank already has an independent cache            |
| CUDA graphs and kernel fusion            | Standard, though model-specific tuning still matters               |
| FlashAttention, FlashInfer and FlashMLA  | Available engine backends                                          |
| FP8 and other quantized kernels          | Supported, with maturity varying by model/hardware                 |
| TP, DP, EP, PP and context parallelism   | Standard multi-GPU mechanisms                                      |
| Speculative decoding/MTP                 | Available; exact-model correctness still requires testing          |
| Prefill/decode disaggregation            | Available and useful for independently controlling TTFT and ITL    |
| Basic autoscaling, queueing and spot use | Infrastructure practice rather than proprietary serving technology |

vLLM already exposes continuous batching, chunked prefill, prefix caching, CUDA graphs, optimized attention/MoE kernels, speculative decoding, P/D disaggregation, and multiple parallelism modes. It also documents that cache-aware routing between data-parallel ranks can improve prefix-cache reuse. ([vLLM][16])

## 6. What could become proprietary

The defensible layer is the control system built on top of the commodity engine:

1. **Deadline-aware admission and scheduling.** Predict input length, output length, phase cost, MTP acceptance, and remaining slack; decide when to batch, start, migrate, or reject a request.

2. **Marginal-cost fleet routing.** Optimize across exact checkpoint revision, hardware type, provider price, interruption probability, data residency, cache locality, and fallback state.

3. **Cache-value estimation.** Admit and evict prefixes based on predicted reuse, HBM opportunity cost, transfer cost, tenant isolation, and the effect on batch capacity.

4. **Dynamic prefill/decode pool sizing.** Reallocate capacity according to queue composition while pricing the KV-transfer and tail-latency consequences.

5. **Spot recovery with deadline preservation.** Checkpoint request state, transfer or recompute KV economically, and select on-demand fallback based on remaining slack.

6. **Identity and quality enforcement.** Continuously verify model manifests, tokenizer output, deterministic canaries, sampler distributions, and runtime regressions.

7. **Trace-derived pricing.** Estimate the true marginal cost of each request before accepting it rather than applying a fixed average batching discount.

These become proprietary only if they produce repeatable savings over ordinary continuous batching and SLO-aware routing. An internal scheduler with no measured advantage is not a moat.

## 7. Three experiments Arcten can run within two weeks

### Experiment 1 — Exact-FP8 performance frontier

**Purpose:** determine whether any proposed tier clears its break-even threshold.

**Configuration**

* Pin the exact model revision and complete identity manifest.
* Compare 8×H200 and 8×B200.
* Test vLLM 0.25.1 and SGLang 0.5.16, plus any required pinned upstream GLM-5.2 fixes.
* FP8 weights and FP8 E4M3 KV only.
* Test MTP depths 0, 1, 3 and 5.
* Inputs: 8K, 32K, 128K, 256K and 1M.
* Outputs: 256, 1K and 4K.
* Cache off, then controlled cache-hit ratios.
* Concurrency and open-loop arrival-rate sweeps through saturation.

**Report**

* Separate prefill and decode goodput.
* TTFT, TPOT, ITL and E2E at P50/P90/P99.
* GPU SM, tensor-core, HBM, DRAM and NVLink utilization.
* Peak/steady HBM, host RAM and CPU.
* MTP accepted length and acceptance rate.
* OOM, invalid output and retry rates.
* Five or more runs per point, with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals.

**Quality tests**

* Greedy token/logit comparison against an unoptimized exact-FP8 reference.
* Fixed-seed sampling comparisons.
* Distribution tests for stochastic sampling.
* Long-context retrieval, tool calling, structured output and agentic benchmarks.

**Pass gate**

At minimum, exceed the zero-margin table. For a commercially credible result, exceed the relevant 30%-margin threshold with P99 completion inside the tier’s target window.

### Experiment 2 — Deadline-aware scheduler replay

**Purpose:** isolate the value of waiting from ordinary continuous batching.

Compare:

* FIFO continuous batching.
* Earliest-deadline-first.
* Slack-aware batching.
* Predicted-cost/deadline scheduling.
* Cache-aware routing.
* Colocated versus disaggregated prefill/decode.

Replay low-load, synchronized-burst, sustained-load and output-heavy traces. Include Now, one-minute, five-minute and best-effort queues on the same fleet.

**Primary outputs**

* Productive utilization.
* GPU-seconds per million input and output tokens.
* Deadline-miss and refund rate.
* P50/P90/P99 completion time.
* Extra node count required during bursts.
* Savings attributable specifically to the delayed window.

**Kill criterion**

Stop treating delay as a differentiator if the five-minute queue reduces full COGS by less than approximately 15% at representative load, or if it requires the same peak warm capacity as Now.

### Experiment 3 — Cache, spot and failure economics

**Purpose:** test the assumptions required by Standard and Flex.

Run 1-, 5- and 15-minute HBM retention, CPU-tier cache, and no-cache baselines. Inject spot termination, capacity-unavailable, node crash, KV-transfer failure and corrupted-worker scenarios. Maintain an on-demand fallback pool of varying sizes.

Measure:

* Cache hits per retained prefix.
* HBM consumed per paid cached token.
* Cache-driven batching loss.
* Restart, model-load and compilation time.
* Wasted compute and duplicate token generation.
* Queue growth and deadline misses during recovery.
* Effective spot discount after retry and fallback reserve.

**Kill criteria**

* Fewer than two paid hits per five-minute Standard prefix.
* Fewer than two spot or three on-demand hits per Flex prefix.
* More than 20% of spot savings consumed by retry/fallback.
* P99 completion targets fail during realistic interruption sequences.

## 8. Three-month engineering recommendation

### Month 1: establish truth

* Build the immutable model identity manifest.
* Implement the exact-FP8 benchmark and quality harness.
* Benchmark B200 against H200; do not assume the more expensive GPU has better dollars per token.
* Instrument phase-level GPU time, HBM, utilization, queue slack and retry cost.
* Obtain real provider quotes for on-demand, committed and interruptible capacity.
* Use cloud credits only for experiments; report COGS at contractual cash prices.

### Month 2: build the control plane

* Implement deadline-aware admission and queueing.
* Add per-request prefill/decode cost prediction.
* Add cache-aware routing and cache-value telemetry.
* Prototype P/D pool resizing.
* Implement interruption injection, restart automation and on-demand fallback.
* Collect design-partner traces through Gateway/BYOK where possible.
* Seek written OEM/wholesale permission for any provider-based fallback.

### Month 3: narrow pilot

Launch only after representative traces meet all of these gates:

* At least **30% gross margin after the complete COGS model**, not GPU cost alone.
* At least 80–85% productive utilization during the committed operating period.
* P99 completion within the marketed target under burst and failure tests.
* Stable exact-FP8 quality across the full runtime matrix.
* Cache reuse sufficient for the cached-input price.
* Cold-start and interruption reserves included in pricing.
* A minimum-spend or anchor-volume arrangement sufficient to keep the checkpoint warm.
* Written commercial rights for every non-self-hosted fallback route.

### Product sequence

1. **Gateway/BYOK for trace collection and demand discovery.**
2. **Now** only for customers or aggregate demand sufficient to keep capacity warm.
3. **Experimental Priority**, advertised initially as a percentile or average completion target, not a hard one-minute deadline.
4. Hold **Standard** until exact-FP8 throughput or contracted compute lowers the lower-bound price floor materially.
5. Hold **Flex** until spot/fallback experiments demonstrate at least a 30% margin after interruption reserve.

Do not launch all four tiers simultaneously. The current evidence supports market testing of the scheduler, not a four-tier price commitment.

## 10. Explicit reasons the approach might fail

1. **Insufficient compatible demand.** A 756 GB checkpoint cannot be kept warm economically at low load.

2. **Delay does not improve saturated economics.** When ordinary continuous batching already fills the GPU, waiting adds queue latency without reducing execution cost.

3. **Bursts remain synchronized.** Customer jobs may naturally arrive on cron, CI, market-close or agent-cycle boundaries, preserving the same peak-capacity requirement.

4. **Exact FP8 underperforms the NVFP4 headlines.** Current public production results cannot be transferred across precision formats.

5. **Long contexts destroy concurrency.** KV memory, indexer work and TTFT increase sharply, while a few 1M-token requests can consume most available HBM.

6. **Output-heavy workloads dominate.** Decode remains sequential and latency-sensitive; the proposed output discounts may be too aggressive.

7. **Spot savings disappear after failures.** Retry compute, cold loading, fallback capacity and deadline refunds can exceed the headline spot discount.

8. **One- and five-minute windows are too short for recovery.** Compilation and loading can exceed the entire completion window.

9. **Cache reuse is lower than assumed.** User-specific prompts, privacy isolation and changing tool state may prevent cross-request prefix reuse.

10. **Cache locality conflicts with cheapest-capacity routing.** Moving requests to a cheaper provider may lose the prefix cache and cost more overall.

11. **Topology changes correctness.** Recent mixed-dtype, MTP, EP and cache bugs show that a configuration can alter quality or corrupt output despite using the same weight files.

12. **Provider capacity or prices change.** List-price spot supply is not a committed fleet.

13. **Retail resale rights are unavailable.** Arcten may be forced to self-host or negotiate wholesale terms.

14. **Thin margins are consumed by non-GPU costs.** Payments, observability, support, abuse prevention, retries, credits/refunds and engineering operations are significant at Priority, Standard and Flex prices.

15. **Customers do not accept probabilistic timing.** Averages may be commercially insufficient when workflows require a deterministic deadline.

16. **The market already prices delay.** Existing batch services offer substantial discounts, albeit with much longer windows, reducing the novelty of “wait for cheaper inference.”
